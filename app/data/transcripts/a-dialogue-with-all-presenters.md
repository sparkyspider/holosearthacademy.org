## Abstract

"In listening to your fellow presenters, what was it that touched you?" With that question, Dr Claudius van Wyk opened the dialogue session.

Prof. Kobus Du Pisani answered first. One word ran through every presentation: hope. He had introduced Jan Smuts as the messenger of hope in the time of anxiety after the Great War, and he listened now as each presenter took up that thread, crediting the gathering with one collective achievement, that it had broken open the concept of holism to show, in essence, a message of hope inside it. The panel attended to hope from different directions:

David Lorimer placed Smuts as a representative man in Emerson's sense, one figure in a hundred years of advancing holism, and held that real hope is *conditional*, earned by action and a new consciousness, a higher frequency, since the system we have and the people we presently are remain unequal to the task, and the same track otherwise repeats.

Dr Jude Currivan took love as foundational to the very nature of the universe; where the room had reached for the language of *paradox* she offered *parallax*, many directions of sight onto one wholeness, so that diversity becomes something to celebrate from a grounding of love and of hope. Her deeper claim was that the underlying narrative itself is changing from separation towards holism, oneness and unity-in-diversity, and that the stories surfacing in the room were coming forward because that narrative is on the move.

Jeff Blumberg, who keeps and will publish the Smuts and Margaret Clark letters, chose to dampen the spirit of hope a little and keep it honest against the record: Smuts asked where the God of holism was at Versailles and never found that God until he died, and confronting Hitler and Mussolini he came face to face with evil and saw that the tendency towards unity did not always mean good. His hope had been evolutionary, the lifelong *alles sal regkom*, faith that evolution would somehow come right. Jeff traced him from an early idea of God as the spirit of the whole, a formulation still carrying a dualism that nagged at him, to his final position in the letter of 27 January 1944, where holism turns out to be love: *God is holism, God is love*.

The discussion then turned to the question of evil. In Smuts as Jeff reads him, evil can never be abstracted from the whole, so one stands on it while climbing towards unity, the way materialism is a phase in holism on the road to the spiritual. David gave evil a cosmic role, an energy to alchemise so that we ascend, and he was scathing about its political twin, the wholesale naming of the demon of the moment, a self-serving fiction in which we cast ourselves as the good guys, carry no shadow, behave as the bad guys all the same, and so, he said, need to grow up. Marcus Link located evil in its not being allowed, a life-force repressed and misread, held outside of the fractal *living range* of 1.3 to 1.5. That fractal range of 1.3 to 1.5, Marcus said, is the breathing space between Apollo's collapse into selfhood and the Dionysian dissolution into chaos; focusing on the ascent alone, he warned, misses that living range and lets the repressed life explode as it either tends towards 1 or 2, the two extremes of the fractal range the collapse into oneness or chaos. Marcus spoke of individuating through his own brokenness, the personal and the political running on one fractal logic. Jude heard even an individuated expression such as Hitler mirroring back to the collective psyche what it had not yet healed, a way-shower for how we move forward together. She named that movement *the weirdo's journey*, *weird* in its Old English sense *the way of wisdom*, the passage from *me* to *we* by which we find each other, and she reframed struggle itself as a dance.

Ora then turned the frame of the gathering towards relinquishing and releasing the *socio-pathological narrative* of good and bad, ascendance and descendance, that it kept duelling with, so that it might drop into a *felt* (a felting space of belonging) listening humbly from inside the same cloth, since we are already conscientious. David answered that we stand in a no-man's-land between stories, that the new story is a new relationship, a *communion of subjects* in Thomas Berry's phrase, and he pointed to Toynbee's *creative minority*, the roughly five per cent working in the margins who formulate the next wave. Marcus found in Cecil Collins' *Fool* a company of those who find themselves living alone in a society, connected in Kairotic time or deep time, and said the festival had lifted something from his shoulders, turning despair towards joy.

Then the floor spoke, in several distinct voices. Ross Olson offered the Lakota *Mitákuye Oyás'iŋ*, "all my relations", the same spiritual principle in other words, a holism reaching back through every ancestor and forward through all the generations to come; hope without action, he said, just stays inside you, so he keeps pointing the finger back at himself, holding gently that most of humanity has not yet come to this awareness, that this is okay, that it is the way it is supposed to be, and that it need not stay that way. Rhett Gayle, on the eve of Pentecost, named his own interest as turning the lead of human suffering into the gold of wisdom, drew on the early Christian path of *theosis*, and offered Christianity as a resource with real power in it, a story whose truth he set to one side, carried by an asymmetry in which God seeks us so strongly that *God shows up at the party*, the incarnation of a God transcendent and at the same time present among us as a person. Tarryn van Wyk spoke for the relief of finding deep intellectual research and passion beneath the love and light, and so many people on the same wavelength, thanking the presenters for doing the work themselves and hoping it would ripple further out. Berry Behr was struck by the timing, the festival speaking into missing pieces of the *trans-disciplinary, trans-civilisational* book she is herself writing, moved that the voice of Africa, Smuts as a son of Africa, was entering a conversation beyond space and time, an architecture of bridging and a sense of collective emergence.

Marcus ended the exchange by giving David back a book David had once recommended to him, P.W. Martin's *Experiment in Depth*, with Eliot's line about arriving where we began and knowing the place for the first time. Michael, closing, summarised: the hope that matters is *authentic hope*, there is the *parallax* view, and there is Smuts as a representative man.

And yet we behave as the bad guys as well, and we can't be trusted either. We need to grow up. It makes good news, it makes good propaganda, it brings people together — but at the same time, it simply perpetuates a pattern which is ultimately self-defeating and unconscious.

### Marcus — the Fool, and finding the cave-dwellers' community

**Marcus Link:** My response goes back to the early 2000s, when there was a book — sadly, I forget the author — called *Cultural Creatives*. It was only then, through my therapy, that I found the English painter Cecil Collins' work on the Fool.
